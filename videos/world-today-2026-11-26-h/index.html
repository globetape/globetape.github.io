◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 2 MIN READ

World news on the map: 26 November 2026

The international headlines of 26 November 2026, mapped from west to east.

United States · Storm: Storm delays macy's 100th parade

We start in New York, where a storm postponed the Macy's Thanksgiving parade for the first time in 100 years. Wind and rain hit the East Coast on Thursday, leaving over 900,000 homes and businesses without power. The parade moves to Saturday.

Brazil · Economy: Banker to run brazil's finances

Now to Brazil, where Bradesco chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco accepted Lula's offer to run the finance ministry. Investors welcomed the pick: the Ibovespa jumped 6.07 percent, its biggest daily gain since just after the election's first round. Trabuco, 75, will lead the ministry in Lula's next term.

United Kingdom · Migration: Uk net migration at 1997 low

Over to Britain, where net migration fell to an estimated 77,000 in the year to June. That is down from 204,000 a year earlier, and the lowest since 1997 outside the pandemic. Prime Minister Andy Burnham welcomed the figures, but said there is more work to do.

Dr Congo · Ebola: Congo's ebola cases pass 12,000

Now to eastern DR Congo, where confirmed Ebola cases have passed 12,000, government data showed on Thursday. Of 12,021 confirmed cases, 5,904 people have died. The Bundibugyo virus has no approved vaccine, and fighting near Bunia is hampering the response. It is the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record.

Kuwait · Iran: Drone attack in kuwait kills two

Over to Kuwait, where a drone attack killed two Italian technicians at an air-defence site on Thursday. Kuwait and Italy blamed Iranian drones; a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani were also wounded. Iran denied involvement, and its foreign minister offered to help clarify what happened.

New Zealand · Election: Nz coalition one seat short

Finally, New Zealand, where the official count left Christopher Luxon's coalition one seat short of a majority. His three parties have 65 seats, as many as the opposition and centrist Opportunity combined. Luxon will now court Opportunity; without a deal, voters could go back to the polls.
