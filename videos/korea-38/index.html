◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 3 MIN READ

Why the Korean War Is Still Only on Pause

The Korean War has been on pause since 1953. The fighting stopped on 27 July 1953 with an armistice, a military ceasefire, but no peace treaty has ever been signed. The border it left behind is not the 38th parallel either: it is the line where the front froze.

Where did the 38th parallel come from?

In August 1945, as the Second World War was ending, Korea was split in two at the 38th parallel. The line was proposed on the night of 10 to 11 August 1945 by two US colonels, Charles Bonesteel and Dean Rusk, working at the Pentagon. According to Rusk's memorandum, the aim was to include the capital of Korea in the area of responsibility of American troops.

Under General Order No. 1, approved on 15 August 1945, the United States would accept the Japanese surrender south of the parallel and the Soviet Union north of it. Soviet troops reached the line later that month; American troops landed in September.

How did the Korean War start?

On 25 June 1950, North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel. What followed was a war of movement:

  • Summer 1950: the front swung south, down to the Pusan perimeter.
  • Autumn 1950: after the Inchon landing on 15 September, the front swung north, close to the Yalu River.
  • Winter 1950 to 1951: after China intervened on 25 October 1950, the front moved back south, and Seoul fell again on 4 January 1951.

From mid-1951, the front settled near the 38th parallel, and it stayed there for the rest of the war.

Did the Korean War ever end?

The fighting ended. The war, formally, did not. On 27 July 1953 an armistice was signed at Panmunjom, at 10:00, and it came into force at 22:00 that night.

It was signed by Lieutenant General William K. Harrison Jr. for the United Nations Command and General Nam Il for the Korean People's Army and the Chinese People's Volunteers. In short: North Korea, China and the U.N. Command signed it. South Korea did not. Its president at the time, Syngman Rhee, refused.

The armistice is a military document. Its own text calls for a cessation of hostilities "until a final peaceful settlement is achieved". That settlement never came: a conference in Geneva in 1954 failed, and according to the United Nations Command, a permanent peace treaty has never been signed, so the Korean Peninsula technically remains in a state of war.

The question is still open. In August 2026, South Korea's president, Lee Jae Myung, proposed talks "to officially end the war".

Is the border between North and South Korea the 38th parallel?

No. The border set in 1953 is not the 1945 line: it is where the front stood when the fighting stopped.

That line crosses the 38th parallel rather than following it. In the west, it runs south of the parallel, so the city of Kaesong, which lies south of the 38th, ended up in North Korea. In the east, the border runs north of the parallel, so a mountainous area above the 38th belongs to South Korea.

What is the DMZ?

Around the line runs the Demilitarized Zone, or DMZ. It is 4 kilometres wide: 2 kilometres on each side of the Military Demarcation Line, the line where the front froze in 1953. It still separates the two Koreas today.

Key takeaways

  • Korea was split at the 38th parallel in August 1945, on a line proposed by two US officers.
  • The Korean War began on 25 June 1950, when North Korean forces crossed the parallel.
  • An armistice stopped the fighting on 27 July 1953; North Korea, China and the U.N. Command signed it, South Korea did not.
  • No peace treaty has ever been signed, so the war is on pause rather than formally over.
  • Today's border is the 1953 front line, which crosses the 38th parallel, surrounded by a 4 km wide DMZ.

Questions about this tape

Is the Korean War still going on?

The fighting stopped on 27 July 1953 with an armistice, but no peace treaty has ever been signed. According to the United Nations Command, the Korean Peninsula technically remains in a state of war.

Who signed the Korean War armistice?

The armistice was signed by the United Nations Command, North Korea and China at Panmunjom on 27 July 1953. South Korea did not sign it.

Is the North Korea–South Korea border the 38th parallel?

No. The border is the front line where the fighting stopped in 1953, and it crosses the 38th parallel: it runs south of it in the west and north of it in the east.
