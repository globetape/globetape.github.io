◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 3 MIN READ

Ukraine's Front Line from 2014 to 2026, Month by Month

As of 17 November 2026, Russia controls about 19% of Ukraine, according to DeepStateMap: roughly 114,000 km², including Crimea. That is an area larger than the whole of Portugal. The figure is the result of more than a decade of change, from the annexation of Crimea in 2014 to the full-scale invasion of 2022 and the slow movement of the front line since.

All front-line figures on this page refer to the date given. The line keeps moving, so later figures may differ.

How much of Ukraine does Russia control today?

In the DeepStateMap snapshot of 17 November 2026, Russia controlled 114,449 km², or 19.2% of Ukraine including Crimea. Measured against Ukraine's official area of 603,550 km², the share is 19.0%.

Other trackers give similar figures. Russia Matters, using DeepStateMap data to 11 November 2026, put it at about 45,227 square miles, roughly 117,100 km², or "about 19% of Ukraine". The Council on Foreign Relations' Global Conflict Tracker describes it as roughly 20 percent.

For scale: Portugal covers 92,225 km² including its islands. The area under Russian control in November 2026 is larger than all of it.

What happened in 2014?

In 2013, the whole of Ukraine, including Crimea, was under Ukrainian control. That changed in 2014.

  • March 2014: Russia annexed Crimea. The annexation treaty was signed on 18 March 2014.
  • April 2014: Russian troops and Russian-backed local forces took control of part of the Donbas, in eastern Ukraine.
  • February 2015: after the fighting around Debaltseve, the line in the Donbas became largely stable.

What changed in 2022?

On 24 February 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, with forces advancing from the north, the east and the south.

Russian control peaked in March 2022. Estimates of that peak vary by source, from about 21% of the country (around 133,000 km², according to ISW) to 27% (according to Al Jazeera). Either way, Russia held over a fifth of Ukraine at that point.

Where did Ukraine retake territory?

Ukraine pushed back in three main areas during 2022:

  • Around Kyiv: Russian forces withdrew from the north in April 2022.
  • Around Kharkiv: a Ukrainian counteroffensive in September 2022 retook ground in the region.
  • Around Kherson: Ukraine retook Kherson on 11 November 2022.

By December 2022, the share of Ukraine under Russian control had fallen to 17.7%.

How has the front line moved since 2023?

Since then, the changes have been gradual. Month by month, Russian forces have advanced slowly, village by village, notably on the Donetsk front. The share of Ukraine under Russian control rose from 17.7% in December 2022 to 19.2% in November 2026.

The front line is still moving, which is why every figure on this page is tied to a date.

Key takeaways

  • As of 17 November 2026, Russia controls about 19% of Ukraine (114,449 km², including Crimea), according to DeepStateMap.
  • That area is larger than Portugal (92,225 km²).
  • Russia annexed Crimea in March 2014; Russian-backed forces took part of the Donbas the same year.
  • The full-scale invasion began on 24 February 2022; at its peak in March 2022, Russia held over a fifth of the country.
  • Ukraine retook ground around Kyiv, Kharkiv and Kherson in 2022, bringing the figure to 17.7% by December 2022.
  • Since then, the share has risen slowly to 19.2% by November 2026.
