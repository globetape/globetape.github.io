◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 2 MIN READ

How Lesotho Ended Up Surrounded by South Africa

Lesotho is inside South Africa because of a decision taken more than 150 years ago. It is a fully independent country of 30,355 km², about the size of Belgium, and South Africa surrounds it completely. In 1868 its king asked Britain for protection; in 1910 the territory, then called Basutoland, stayed outside the Union of South Africa; and on 4 October 1966 it became independent as Lesotho.

Where exactly is Lesotho?

Look at a map of southern Africa and you will spot a hole in the middle of South Africa. That hole is not a lake or a nature reserve. It is a separate country with its own government and its own borders: Lesotho.

What makes it unusual is that it has only one neighbour. Every kilometre of its border is shared with South Africa. Geographers call this an enclave: a country entirely surrounded by a single other country.

How big is Lesotho?

Lesotho covers 30,355 km². Belgium covers 30,689 km². The two are almost exactly the same size.

So Lesotho is not a tiny city-state tucked into a corner of the map. It is a country the size of a European nation, sitting completely inside its larger neighbour.

Why is Lesotho's lowest point a world record?

Lesotho holds a genuine world record. Its lowest point is about 1,400 metres above sea level, the highest lowest point of any country on Earth.

In practical terms, there is nowhere in Lesotho that sits lower than roughly 1,400 metres. Even the bottom of the country is high ground: the whole of Lesotho sits well above sea level.

Why did Lesotho never become part of South Africa?

The answer is in its history. Three dates explain most of it:

  • 1868: King Moshoeshoe I asked Britain for protection. The territory became the British protectorate of Basutoland.
  • 1910: The Union of South Africa came together, and Basutoland stayed outside it.
  • 4 October 1966: Basutoland became independent and took the name Lesotho.

British protection put Basutoland on a separate track from its neighbours. When the Union of South Africa was formed in 1910, Basutoland was not part of it, and it never joined afterwards. By the time it became independent in 1966, the borders were already set. The result is the map we see today: an independent country with a single neighbour on every side.

Are there other countries completely surrounded by another country?

Yes, but very few. Only three countries on Earth are entirely surrounded by one other country. Lesotho is one of them. The other two are San Marino and Vatican City.

That makes Lesotho a true geographic rarity: one of just three countries in the world that cannot reach any other nation without first crossing a single neighbour.

  • Lesotho is an independent country completely surrounded by South Africa.
