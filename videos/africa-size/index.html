◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 3 MIN READ

Mercator vs Reality: How Big Africa Actually Is

Africa covers about 30.4 million km², roughly 14 times the area of Greenland, even though the two look about the same size on the world map most people know. Africa is so large that the mainland United States, India and all 27 countries of the European Union fit inside it at the same time, and almost half of the continent is still left uncovered.

Why does Africa look smaller than it is?

The map most of us grew up with uses the Mercator projection, drawn by Gerardus Mercator in 1569 for navigators. To flatten a round globe into a rectangle, it stretches everything that lies far from the equator.

The stretching grows fast as you move towards the poles. At 60 degrees of latitude, areas look about 4 times bigger than they really are. At 72 degrees, they look about 10.5 times bigger.

Mercator has never gone away. Online maps such as Google Maps use a version of it called Web Mercator, so it is still the picture of the world many people see every day.

Africa lies across the equator, where Mercator distorts least. Greenland, Canada and Russia lie far to the north, where it distorts most.

Is Greenland really as big as Africa?

No. On a Mercator map Greenland looks about as big as Africa, but that is the projection at work.

Greenland covers 2,166,086 km². Africa covers about 30.4 million km². Divide one by the other and you get roughly 14. Slide Greenland down to the equator, where the map stops stretching it, and it shrinks into a small patch inside Africa.

What countries fit inside Africa?

To show how big Africa really is, we placed three large pieces of the world inside it, all at once and without overlapping:

  • The mainland United States: 8,080,464 km², meaning the 48 contiguous states plus DC, without Alaska or Hawaii.
  • India: 3,287,263 km².
  • The European Union: 4,236,351 km² across all 27 member states.

Together they add up to 15,604,078 km², about 51% of Africa. That leaves roughly 49% of the continent, almost half, still uncovered.

How was the fit checked?

The comparison is not an artist's sketch. The fit was measured on Natural Earth outlines, moving each country across the globe without stretching or distorting its shape, only allowing it to rotate.

A few choices keep it fair:

  • Africa means the mainland only, without Madagascar, small islands or the Sinai Peninsula.
  • The United States means the 48 contiguous states.
  • India is shown without the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
  • EU countries are shown with their European territory only, without the Canary Islands, the Azores, Madeira or overseas regions.

With those outlines, all 29 pieces fit inside mainland Africa with 0 km² sticking out past the coast and 0 km² of overlap between them.

Key takeaways

  • Africa covers about 30.4 million km², around 14 times the size of Greenland.
  • The Mercator projection (1569) stretches land far from the equator, which is why Greenland looks as big as Africa.
  • The mainland US, India and all 27 EU countries fit inside Africa at the same time.
  • Those three together cover about 51% of Africa, leaving almost half uncovered.
  • The fit was measured on real outlines, with no overlap and nothing outside the coast.
