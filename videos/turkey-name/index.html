The Turkey Isn't From Türkiye, India or Peru

Türkiye · 26 November 2026 · 0:29

THE SHORT ANSWER

Why is a turkey called a turkey? It's a North American bird, yet English names it like the country, Turkish (hindi) and French (dinde) name it after India, Dutch after Calicut and Portuguese after Peru. Every farm turkey traces back to Mexico.

◀◀ DEEP DIVE · 3 MIN READ

One Bird, Many Wrong Countries: How the Turkey Got Its Names

A turkey is called a turkey because of a mix-up. In the 1540s, English speakers used the name for the guinea fowl, which arrived through Ottoman territory, then known in English as Turkey. In the 1550s the name passed to the American bird, which people confused with it. The turkey is actually native to North America, and every farm turkey today traces back to Mexico.

Why is a turkey called a turkey?

The English word comes from the place name for the Ottoman lands. The name was first given to the guinea fowl, a bird that arrived through Ottoman territory. When the American bird reached Europe, the two were confused, and by the 1550s turkey had moved to the new arrival.

Exactly why the confusion happened is still debated. That the name comes from the country is not.

What is a turkey called in other languages?

English is not alone. Many languages name the bird after somewhere it never came from.

  • Turkish: hindi, meaning "Indian" or "of India", from Hind or Hindistan. It was probably modelled on the French word.
  • French: dinde, from poule d'Inde or coq d'Inde, "hen" or "cock of India", recorded between 1542 and 1552. Like the English name, it was first used for the guinea fowl.
  • Dutch: kalkoen, from Calkoensche hinne, "hen of Calicut", recorded between 1567 and 1573. Calicut is the Indian port now called Kozhikode, in Kerala.
  • Portuguese: peru. Dictionaries say it probably comes from the place name Peru, which at the time was used for all of Spanish America.

That gives three names pointing to India, one to the lands of today's Türkiye, and one, probably, to Peru.

Why do so many names point to India?

The trail starts with French. There, coq d'Inde first described the guinea fowl and then moved to the turkey when the Spanish brought the bird from Mexico. The Turkish hindi likely followed the French model. The Dutch name went further and pointed to one specific Indian port, Calicut.

Where do turkeys really come from?

The wild turkey, Meleagris gallopavo, is native to North America: the United States, Mexico and southern Canada.

The farm turkey has a more precise origin. According to a genetic study by Speller and colleagues, published in PNAS in 2010, all of today's domestic turkeys descend from the turkey domesticated in south-central Mexico. The Spanish took it to Europe in the early 16th century.

So the bird on a Thanksgiving table traces back to Mexico, not to Türkiye, India or Peru.

What is a turkey called in Mexico?

In Mexico the bird is called guajolote, a word also used in El Salvador and Honduras. According to the Royal Spanish Academy, it comes from the Nahuatl word huexolotl. Unlike the European names, it does not point to a faraway country: it comes from a language of the land where the bird was first domesticated.

Key takeaways

  • The English turkey comes from the old name for the Ottoman lands, first given to the guinea fowl and then to the American bird.
  • Turkish hindi and French dinde both mean "of India"; Dutch kalkoen points to Calicut, also in India.
  • Portuguese peru probably comes from Peru, then a name for all of Spanish America.
  • The turkey is native to North America, and every domestic turkey descends from birds domesticated in Mexico.
  • In Mexico it is called guajolote, from the Nahuatl huexolotl.
