The Turkey Isn't From Türkiye, India or Peru

English: turkey
After the Ottoman lands
The name was first used in the 1540s for the guinea fowl, which arrived through Ottoman territory, and passed to the American bird in the 1550s through confusion between the two.
SOURCE: Online Etymology Dictionary
Turkish: hindi
"Indian, of India"
From Hind or Hindistan, probably modelled on the French word dinde.
SOURCE: Online Etymology Dictionary
French: dinde
"Of India"
From poule or coq d'Inde, recorded between 1542 and 1552, a name first used for the guinea fowl.
SOURCE: TLFi (CNRTL)
Dutch: kalkoen
After Calicut
From Calkoensche hinne, "hen of Calicut" (1567–1573); the Indian port is now called Kozhikode, in Kerala.
SOURCE: Etymologisch Woordenboek van het Nederlands
Portuguese: peru
Probably after Peru
Dictionaries trace it, with some caution, to the place name Peru, then used for all of Spanish America.
SOURCE: Priberam
Where every farm turkey comes from
South-central Mexico
All of today's domestic turkeys descend from birds domesticated there; the Spanish brought them to Europe in the early 16th century.
SOURCE: Speller et al., PNAS 2010
Mexican Spanish: guajolote
From Nahuatl huexolotl
The word is used in Mexico, El Salvador and Honduras.
SOURCE: RAE

Did the Korean War Ever Really End?

38th parallel proposed
10–11 August 1945
Two US colonels, Charles Bonesteel and Dean Rusk, proposed the line overnight at the Pentagon.
SOURCE: US Office of the Historian
War begins
25 June 1950
North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel.
SOURCE: United Nations Command
Armistice signed
27 July 1953
Signed at 10:00 at Panmunjom; it came into force at 22:00 the same day.
SOURCE: US National Archives
Signatories
U.N. Command, North Korea, China
The armistice is a military agreement; South Korea did not sign it.
SOURCE: BBC
Peace treaty
None signed
A permanent peace treaty has never been signed, so the peninsula technically remains in a state of war.
SOURCE: United Nations Command
Width of the DMZ
4 km
The Demilitarized Zone extends 2 km on each side of the Military Demarcation Line.
SOURCE: US National Archives
Border vs. 38th parallel
Crosses it
The 1953 line runs south of the parallel in the west and north of it in the east.

Kaliningrad: Why Does Russia Own It?

Former name
Königsberg
Until 1945 the city was German, part of East Prussia.
Transferred to the USSR
1945
The Potsdam Agreement of August 1, 1945 placed Königsberg and the northern part of East Prussia under the Soviet Union.
SOURCE: Potsdam Agreement (1945)
Renamed Kaliningrad
July 4, 1946
The city was renamed after Mikhail Kalinin.
Cut off from mainland Russia
1991
The USSR recognised Lithuania's independence on September 6, 1991, and the Soviet Union itself dissolved in December 1991.
Neighbours in NATO and the EU
By 2004
Poland joined NATO in 1999 and Lithuania on March 29, 2004; both joined the EU on May 1, 2004.
SOURCE: NATO and EU accession records
Ice-free port
Baltiysk
Home of Russia's Baltic Fleet and Russia's only Baltic port that stays free of ice all year.
SOURCE: US Naval Institute
Area and population
About 15,100 km² · about 1 million people
A small exclave compared with the rest of Russia.

Russia Sold Alaska for 2 Cents an Acre

Purchase price
$7,200,000
The sum the United States agreed to pay Russia for Alaska in 1867.
SOURCE: US Office of the Historian
Treaty signed
March 30, 1867
Signed in Washington by US Secretary of State William Seward and Russian minister Eduard de Stoeckl, at 4 a.m. after negotiating through the night.
SOURCE: US Office of the Historian
Senate vote
37 to 2
The US Senate ratified the treaty on April 9, 1867.
SOURCE: US Office of the Historian
Formal handover
October 18, 1867
In Sitka, the Russian flag was lowered and the US flag was raised.
SOURCE: US Office of the Historian
Price per acre
About 2 cents
Roughly 1,518,800 km² (375 million acres) for $7.2 million works out to about 1.9 cents an acre.
Size compared with France
About 2.7 times
Alaska has about 1,481,346 km² of land, against about 543,940 km² for metropolitan France.
Prudhoe Bay oil discovery
Announced March 12, 1968
Prudhoe Bay is the largest oil field in the United States.
SOURCE: National Park Service
Alaska oil output in 2026
About 413,000–428,000 barrels a day
At around $100 a barrel, that is roughly $7 million worth of oil every four hours.
SOURCE: US Energy Information Administration
